# Audit — October 2026

**Scope:** `7ucky7/espero` at commit `15b907a` (4,983 lines of Python, 78 tests, two paper accounts,
one experimental live path). **Question asked:** is this ready to run unattended, and what would
a factor shop add to it. **Outcome:** ten findings, five of them bugs that change money or a
decision, all fixed in the `audit-and-quant-stack` branch with a regression test each.

The first audit (`AUDIT.md`) found a bot that could not run. This one found a bot that runs
correctly on a good day and had no plan for a bad one.

## Findings

### Bugs that change money or the decision (fixed)

**1. TSMOM paid its fees with money it did not have.** `tsmom.Account.rebalance` invested the full
equity and then subtracted the cost from cash, so cash went negative by exactly the fee after every
rebalance. The XS account had already fixed the identical bug with a two-pass budget; TSMOM had not
received it. Pinned by `test_fully_invested_rebalance_does_not_borrow_the_fees`.

**2. TSMOM could rebalance three times a month.** The "first trading day" window is days 1–3 of the
month on a weekday, and nothing recorded that a rebalance had already happened. Three runs in that
window meant three rebalances, each recomputing signals that can flip across those days. The rule
says one. Now `Account.rebalanced_in_month` checks the database before `rebalance_due` says yes.

**3. The XS universe shrank silently.** `xs.load_prices` swallowed every exception. A Yahoo outage
for twelve coins meant a top-10 chosen from thirty, and the account rebalanced into it without a
word. Separately, `equity()` dropped any held coin that was missing from the price feed, so a
position could vanish from the equity curve. Now: failures are returned and logged; a coin whose last
close is older than two days is not ranked; fewer than 30 rankable coins marks the account but
refuses to rebalance (note `mark (degraded N/42)`); held coins are marked at their last known price,
stored per row.

**4. The live path's risk gate was decorative.** Four separate problems in `live.py`:
- `day_start_equity` was set once and never reset, so the "daily" loss limit was a running loss
  limit since process start
- `_buy` called `check_buy(..., open_positions=0, day_pnl=0.0, ...)`: constants, not state. The
  gate's daily-loss cap could never fire
- SELL signals were ignored; only stop/target exits existed. The backtest and the paper trader exit
  on a SELL signal when in profit or above 0.8 confidence. Live was running a different strategy
  from the one that was tested
- the exit fee was not charged against P&L

All four fixed; `tests/test_live.py::TestRiskPlumbing` covers each. The module is now marked
**experimental** and is not part of the scheduled run.

**5. One unofficial endpoint, zero retries.** Every HTTP call was a bare `urlopen`. Both paper
accounts depend entirely on Yahoo's unofficial chart endpoint. Now `data.http_get` retries with
exponential backoff and jitter on 429/5xx/connection errors (not on other 4xx), and `yahoo_daily`
writes a CSV cache on success and falls back to it, with a warning, when the cache is under three
days old. Used by Coinbase, FRED, Yahoo, OKX, Hyperliquid, Polymarket and Deribit fetches.

### Deployment gaps (closed)

**6. Nothing ran the bot.** Both accounts were CLI commands someone had to remember to invoke. Now
`espero daily` runs tsmom → xs → dashboard → `health.json`, and `scripts/install-launchd.sh` installs
a LaunchAgent that runs it every morning. Steps are isolated: one failure does not stop the others.

**7. A failed run was invisible.** Prints only. Now a rotating log in `~/Library/Logs/espero/`, a
machine-readable `health.json` with `last_success` and per-step errors, macOS notifications and an
optional webhook on failure and on every rebalance.

**8. Unpinned dependencies under pinned numbers.** `numpy>=1.24, pandas>=2.0` with no upper bound,
while `test_reference.py` pins the audit table to four decimals. Now `<3` / `<4`, ruff in CI, a
weekly scheduled CI run to catch drift early.

**9. Database hygiene.** `paper.db` and `live.db` were not gitignored; `features.db` is committed and
kept on purpose as a fixture. Now `*.db` is ignored with that one exception.

**10. Two runs could collide.** SQLite was opened without WAL or a busy timeout. Now WAL + 5 s
timeout on every account database, plus a pid lock file around the daily run.

### Correct as designed (kept)

Causal indicators with a truncation test; next-bar fills; the point-in-time `features.attach`
rule; the risk gate below the strategy; `client_order_id` idempotency; the kill switch;
pre-registration before data; the pinned reference numbers. The new modules follow the same rules:
every `ts_*` operator has the truncation test, the forward return is computed in one named place,
and no new rule touches a live account without its own pre-registration.

## What a factor shop would add, and what was added

The repo's own `RESULTS_XS20.md` diagnosed why its cross-sectional tests failed: no breadth, one
factor, no risk control, no turnover control. The toolkit the Chinese cross-sectional shops run on
(and WorldQuant before them) is now in the package:

| ingredient | module | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| operator dialect | `ops.py` | Alpha101/191 `ts_*` / `cs_*` operators, causal by construction |
| dated universe | `panel.py` | wide panel, eligibility by history and dollar volume on each date |
| factor zoo | `factors.py` | 12 signed factors, 3 literal Alpha101 formulas, rank-average composite |
| judgement | `factor_eval.py` | rank IC, ICIR, t, IC decay, quantile spread, turnover, purged walk-forward, deflated Sharpe, t > 3 hurdle |
| construction | `portfolio.py` | vol targeting, weight caps, turnover buffer, ex-ante book vol |
| costs | `tca.py` | per-trade cost, annual turnover and drag, shown on the dashboard |

What was not added, and why: breadth (42 coins is the universe a small account can reach), machine
learning for factor combination (with ~40 names × 5 years there are not enough independent
observations to fit one honestly), intraday data, and shorting. Each would be a new hypothesis.

Hypothesis #8 (`research/PREREGISTRATION_MF.md`) is the first use of the stack on the repo's own
terms: three factors, rank-averaged, vol-targeted, turnover-buffered, with a weakened holdout and
four pass criteria including the t > 3 IC hurdle. Its result is in `research/RESULTS_MF.md`.

## Not done

- **Venue reconciliation and exchange-side stops** for the live path. Out of scope by decision;
  live stays experimental and off the schedule.
- **Breadth.** The factor stack is only as good as 42 survivors. A dated listing/delisting file would
  be the next data-layer improvement.
- **The forward paper trade** for #8, if it passes, needs a third weekly account. The code for the
  rule exists in `research/multifactor.py`; the account wrapper does not yet.
